Expert Survey on FOMC Communication

Forecasting the June 2026 FOMC meeting

This short survey is part of a research project on on expectations and Federal Reserve communication. We are asking a small group of experts to forecast what the Committee will publish at its upcoming meeting.

What we're asking. You'll forecast the two documents the Committee releases on June 17, 2026: the post-meeting policy statement and the Summary of Economic Projections (SEP). It takes about 10–15 minutes.

How it works. This is a simple web form. As you answer, your responses are saved in your browser, so you can close the page and return later in the same browser without losing your progress. Nothing is sent until you click Submit, when your answers are added to a private Google Sheet used only for this study.

Privacy. This survey is fully anonymous. We ask for and collect no personal information, only your answers, so your responses cannot be traced back to you.

When you're finished, click Submit.

Median projections (SEP)

In the SEP, growth and inflation are Q4-over-Q4, the unemployment rate is the fourth-quarter level, and the federal funds rate is the year-end level. C

Uncertainty

How will the 19 participants assess the uncertainty around their projections?

In the SEP, each participant judges whether the uncertainty around their own projections is lower than, broadly similar to, or higher than the average over the past 20 years. Roughly, broadly similar means the participant sees the width of the historical fan-chart confidence interval as about right for their forecast; higher means they would draw a wider interval, lower a narrower one. For each variable, give the number of the 19 participants you expect in each category; the three numbers add up to 19. Each grey figure is the corresponding count from the March 2026 SEP.
D

Balance of risks

How will the participants assess the balance of risks to those projections?

In the SEP, each participant judges whether the risks to their own projections are weighted to the downside, broadly balanced, or weighted to the upside. Roughly, broadly balanced means viewing the confidence interval around the projection as approximately symmetric, rather than tilted up or down. For each variable, give the number of the 19 participants you expect in each category; the three numbers add up to 19. Each grey figure is the corresponding count from the March 2026 SEP.
